## 1. Summary of the manuscript

The paper studies counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) of contraction metrics for continuous-time autonomous systems ẋ = f(x) on a compact box X. A learner proposes a metric M\_θ = αI + L\_θᵀL\_θ, with L\_θ lower triangular and linear in fixed features. A grid oracle with a certified Lipschitz constant L\_h either certifies the contraction matrix inequality C\_θ(x) ⪯ 0 on X or returns the grid points that violate the grid margin −L\_hτ. The loop, called CEGIS-C, retrains on the returned points with a hinge loss.

The paper makes five claims. First, the grid oracle and the loop are sound (Propositions 1 and 2), and Lemmas 1 to 3 give certified Lipschitz and class constants for polynomial dynamics and polynomial features with n = 2. Second, the contraction condition is homogeneous in M, so certifiability under a scale-equivariant oracle depends only on the shape of M (Lemma 4). In a scale-closed family the raw test admits an infinite scale chase (Corollary 2), which motivates the trace-normalized test g̃\_θ = λ\_max(C\_θ)/tr M\_θ. Third, a fixed training margin below the oracle margin admits a stall at a non-certified candidate (Lemma 5), which motivates coupling the training margin to the oracle requirement. Fourth, a certified uniform margin η improves the contraction rate to λ + η/(2b) (Theorem 1) and absorbs a model residual with magnitude and slope bounds (δ\_0, δ\_1) (Theorem 2).

Fifth, two-dimensional experiments support the analysis. On a polynomial benchmark with a known pullback metric, a fixed margin on a coarse grid stalls (19 of 20 seeds), a coupled margin on the same grid makes the certified Lipschitz bound diverge (20 of 20 seeds), and the normalized coupled-margin loop on a 2001² grid certifies (19 of 20 seeds, median 2 rounds). A residual-robustness experiment exercises Theorem 2. A cost extrapolation shows the exponential growth of the grid oracle in the state dimension. A damped pendulum is certified by a trigonometric-polynomial interval oracle (19 of 20 seeds), and a 6-unit tanh metric is certified by interval bound propagation on an 8001² grid (13 of 20 seeds).

## 2. Overall assessment

The paper is careful and honest, and its verification side is rigorous. Its theorems are elementary, however, and its experiments stay in two dimensions. It needs one substantive theoretical result and a stronger experimental section before it meets the bar of a TAC regular paper.

### Strengths

The problem is timely for the TAC readership: certified contraction metrics for learned models, obtained by a loop that does not depend on polynomial structure.

The verification is rigorous. Exact rational coefficient arithmetic, outward rounding, directed MPFR evaluation, explicit certified Lipschitz constants, and a public, hashed reproduction archive. Few papers in the certificate-learning literature reach this level of numerical care.

The reporting is honest. The paper states what the experiments show and what they do not show (for example lines 296 to 297, 419 to 420, and 515 to 517).

The failure-mode analysis is useful. The stall and the Lipschitz-growth divergence are real pitfalls for anyone who builds a certificate-learning loop, and the paper names them precisely.

Theorem 2 is a clean and usable statement for pipelines with learned dynamics. Remark 4 gives an explicit exchange rate between verified slack and residual size.

### Main weaknesses

1. The theoretical results are elementary for a TAC regular paper. Lemma 4 states the homogeneity of a condition that is linear in M. Lemma 5 states that a learner with zero gradient and zero optimizer state does not move. Theorem 1 is a one-line rearrangement of the matrix inequality. Theorem 2 is a standard perturbation bound (Weyl's inequality plus submultiplicativity). The paper does not overclaim, but the framing "Main results" and "we prove" invites a reviewer to judge the paper by its theorems, and the theorems will not carry it. Remark 2 leaves the one question with real content, relative completeness, open.
2. The theory and the experiments describe different mechanisms. Corollary 2 requires a scale-closed family. The implemented family is not scale-closed (lines 291 to 297), the experiments never exhibit a scale chase, and there is no raw-test ablation. Trace normalization is therefore justified in theory for a situation the experiments do not test, and it is not justified by experiment at all.
3. All synthesis experiments are two-dimensional, and the oracle is a covering-number oracle whose cost the paper itself shows to be prohibitive beyond n of about 3 (Section V-C). The paper names the oracle as the binding constraint (line 522) but does not relax it, although the subbox machinery of Lemma 1 is one step away from an adaptive interval oracle that needs no grid and no Lipschitz constant.
4. The title promises neural certificates. The main study uses a learner that is linear in fixed polynomial features. The neural study is secondary and weaker: 13 of 20 seeds on an 8001² grid, and only 1 of the 13 certified metrics passes at the linear class's grid.
5. Several reported numbers are incomplete. The polynomial study does not report L\_h, L\_hτ, the certified normalized slack s, or how the margin η = 0.0386 of Section V-D was obtained. The 20th seed of two regimes in Figure 1 is unexplained.

### Recommendation

Major revision. The paper becomes a solid TAC paper if it (a) adds a finite-termination or relative-completeness result for a bounded-parameter version of the loop (M1 gives a concrete route), (b) adds the ablations that connect normalization and margin coupling to the experiments (M2), (c) adds an n = 3 example and, if possible, an adaptive interval oracle (M4 and M5), and (d) reports complete certified constants (M8). Items (a) and (b) matter most.

## 3. Major comments

**M1. Add one theoretical result with real content: finite termination of a bounded-parameter version of the loop.** Remark 2 leaves relative completeness open. The standard CEGIS termination argument gives a clean result here, and it also explains the divergence in Figure 1.

Setting. Let X\_τ be a fixed grid with N points. Let Θ be a parameter set on which the certified Lipschitz bound is uniformly bounded, L\_h(θ) ≤ L̄ for all θ in Θ. For the class (4) with features linear in x, C\_θ is quadratic in θ, so the coefficient bounds of Lemma 2 are bounded on any bounded Θ, and a norm ball {‖θ‖ ≤ R} gives an explicit L̄(R). Fix the training margin ε = L̄τ. Assume that the learner returns, at each round, some θ in Θ with zero empirical hinge loss on the accumulated data D whenever such a θ exists (exact minimization, stated as a hypothesis, as the paper already does in Lemma 5). Assume realizability: there is θ⋆ in Θ with g̃\_θ⋆(x) ≤ −L̄τ for all x in X. A grid-level version, g̃\_θ⋆(x\_i) ≤ −L̄τ for all i, suffices if the loop adds no perturbed points.

Argument. At any round, θ⋆ has zero loss on D, so the learner returns some θ with zero loss on D. Every x\_i in D then satisfies g̃\_θ(x\_i) ≤ −L̄τ ≤ −L\_h(θ)τ, so it passes the oracle. Hence every returned violator is a grid point not already in D, and D gains at least one grid point per round. After at most N rounds, D contains the whole grid, and zero loss on D is then exactly the certificate of Definition 2. The loop certifies in at most N rounds.

Consequences. (i) The hypothesis that fails in the coupled-margin coarse-grid experiment is precisely the bound L̄: the certified Lipschitz constant grows by a factor up to 9.9 × 10⁶ (line 425). (ii) The same argument works with the coupled margin ε\_k = γ L\_h(θ\_k)τ under the one-round growth control L\_h(θ\_{k+1}) ≤ γ L\_h(θ\_k), which a trust region on θ enforces. (iii) The bound N is astronomically large and the result says nothing about speed. This is the usual state of CEGIS termination results and should be stated plainly.

Requests. State the result as a proposition after Remark 2. Add a parameter-norm constraint or a trust region to Algorithm 1; a projection step after LEARN suffices. Re-run the coarse-grid coupled-margin experiment with the constraint. The expected outcome is either certification, if the class is realizable at the coarse-grid margin, or a clean non-divergent failure. Either outcome is more informative than unbounded growth, and the result turns an observed pathology into a theorem-backed design rule.

**M2. Close the gap between the scale-chase theory and the experiments.** Three requests.

(a) Add an ablation on the implemented class: the raw test g\_θ with a fixed margin, and the raw test with a coupled margin, both on the operating grid of 2001² points. If the raw loop certifies as often as the normalized loop, state this and present normalization as a theoretical safeguard rather than as a necessary ingredient. If it does not, the experiment finally supports the design choice.

(b) Exhibit the scale chase on a scale-closed family. The simplest choice removes the fixed floor: M\_θ = L\_θᵀL\_θ with a constant term in L\_θ and the lower bound a enforced by a constraint on θ, or a learned α. Corollary 2 then applies, and the raw loop should show the growth of c\_k that the proof constructs. One figure, raw versus normalized test on this family, would make Section IV-A concrete.

(c) Align the abstract and the contribution list with lines 291 to 297. The abstract (lines 8 to 11) and the second contribution (lines 73 to 78) read as if normalization were necessary for the reported experiments. The body says the opposite. State in both places that the scale chase is a property of scale-closed families and that the implemented class is not one.

**M3. State that both failure modes are consequences of oracle incompleteness, and add a grid refinement rule.** Under the hypotheses of Corollary 2 (g\_0 < 0 on X) and of Lemma 5 (sup\_X h\_θ ≤ −ε < 0), the rejected candidate is already a valid contraction metric at rate λ. The loop fails to certify, not to find. Two consequences follow. First, the natural remedy is to reduce τ or to tighten L\_h, and Algorithm 1 has no refinement rule. Second, margin coupling and normalization push the learner toward shapes with a larger slack-to-Lipschitz ratio, which is a different goal from finding a contraction metric; the paper comes close to saying this at lines 436 to 437 and should say it plainly. Request: add a refinement rule to Algorithm 1 (for example, halve τ when the learner fails to meet the coupled margin for r consecutive rounds, within a fixed evaluation budget), or add a paragraph that explains why the grid is kept fixed. With a refinement rule, the coarse-grid experiment of Section V-B would refine and certify instead of diverging, which is the behavior a user wants.

**M4. Complement the covering-number oracle with an adaptive interval oracle for the polynomial class.** Lemma 1 already subdivides X into subboxes and recenters the polynomial entries on each subbox. The same machinery gives, on each subbox, an interval enclosure of C\_θ(x) and therefore an upper bound on λ\_max(C\_θ). One sound bound is λ\_max(C\_θ(x)) ≤ λ\_max(C\_θ(x\_c)) + ‖C\_θ(x) − C\_θ(x\_c)‖\_F, where x\_c is the subbox center and the second term is the recentered coefficient bound without its constant term. Gershgorin discs on the interval matrix give another. Checking λ\_max ≤ −η on every subbox, with bisection of the subboxes that fail, is a sound oracle with no grid, no τ, and no Lipschitz constant, and its cost follows the geometry of the slack rather than a uniform covering. This is the direction the paper names as the binding constraint (lines 445 to 448 and 520 to 522), and for the polynomial class it is a small step from the existing code. It would remove the extrapolation of Section V-C, make an n = 3 example feasible, and give a direct point of comparison with the interval verification of \[12\]. If this is out of scope, add a clear statement of why the grid oracle was preferred over box-wise enclosure, given that the subbox machinery exists.

**M5. Add at least one example with n = 3.** The paper's own extrapolation gives about 2 × 10⁸ grid points at n = 3, from (1 + B√n L\_h/s)^n with the median ratio 0.0057, which is inside the 10⁹ budget of line 442. The sentence "all synthesis experiments remain two-dimensional" (line 517) names a weakness that the paper can remove. A polynomial example from the contraction literature, for instance one of the examples in \[13\], would also allow a direct comparison with sum-of-squares synthesis.

**M6. Exercise the learned-dynamics setting that motivates the paper.** The abstract and the introduction motivate the work by learned models (lines 14 to 16, 29 to 31, and 350 to 351). Theorem 2 is the right tool, but the experiment in Section V-D uses a synthetic polynomial residual with known bounds. Add an experiment in which f is a surrogate fitted to trajectory data of a known system (polynomial regression suffices) and the residual bounds (δ\_0, δ\_1) are certified from the fitting error on X; for a polynomial residual, Lemma 1 gives them. Otherwise, soften the claim to "the theorem is exercised with a known residual".

**M7. Reconsider the term "neurosymbolic" and the title.** In the main study the learner is linear in fixed polynomial features; it is not a neural network. The neural metric appears only in Section V-F, with weaker outcomes. A TAC reviewer will ask why a paper titled "Neurosymbolic Synthesis" presents the neural case as a secondary experiment on a finer grid. Two options. (a) Promote the neural study: same controlled benchmark, same grid, the ablations of M2, and an off-the-shelf verifier (α,β-CROWN or similar, as in \[15\]) next to the custom interval propagation. (b) Retitle, for example "Counterexample-Guided Synthesis of Contraction Certificates with a Certified Verification Oracle", and present the neural case as an extension. Option (b) matches the content as it stands. If the title is kept, the sentence at lines 90 to 91 ("We use the term neurosymbolic for this learner-verifier architecture") needs a justification, since the literature uses the term for neural learners.

**M8. Report the certified constants for every study and explain the unexplained seeds.** For the polynomial study the paper reports b (median 4.26 over certified seeds, line 407) but not L\_h, L\_hτ, the certified normalized slack s, or the raw margin η that enters Theorem 1. Section V-D reports η = 0.0386 for one candidate without saying whether η was obtained from Remark 3 (η = n a s) or from the raw test on the grid. Add one table with, per study: grid size, τ, L\_h (median and range over certified seeds), L\_hτ, s, η, a, b, the improved rate λ′, and the overshoot √(b/a). This table also lets the reader check the consistency of Figure 1 with Lemma 3. State what happened to the seed that did not certify on the operating grid (lines 426 to 427) and to the seed that did not stall under the fixed margin (line 421).

**M9. Positioning with respect to related work (specifics in Section 6).** Three points matter most. (i) The grid oracle with a Lipschitz margin is a sampling-based verification of the type introduced by Bobiti and Lazar (IEEE TAC, 2018) for Lyapunov conditions; cite it and state the difference. (ii) Theorem 2 should be positioned against work on contraction metrics under model error (Chou, Ozay, and Berenson, CDC 2021) and on certificates for learned dynamics (Zhou, Quartz, De Sterck, and Liu, NeurIPS 2022). (iii) The comparison with sum-of-squares for the pendulum (lines 66 to 67 and 463 to 464) should acknowledge that recasting sin x\_1 and cos x\_1 as variables s and c with the constraint s² + c² = 1 is an exact and standard reformulation, not an approximation. The advantage of the interval oracle is generality (any C² vector field, no semidefinite program, no post-verification of a floating-point SDP solution), not that sum-of-squares cannot treat the pendulum.

**M10. Consider training on the certifiability gap directly.** Definition 3 introduces Γ(M\_θ) = max\_i h(x\_i) + L\_h(θ)τ, which is exactly the quantity the oracle tests. Algorithm 1 freezes L\_h from the previous round inside ε. For the class (4), L\_h(θ) is an explicit function of θ (a root of sums of squares of sums of absolute values of polynomials in θ), so the learner can minimize the hinge on Γ directly, max{0, g̃\_θ(x) + γ L\_h(θ)τ}, with a floating-point surrogate of the coefficient bound for training and the exact bound for verification. This removes the open-loop margin coupling and penalizes Lipschitz growth during training rather than after it. Either implement it as a further regime or explain why it was not done.

## 4. Technical comments by section

All proofs that I checked are correct. The comments below concern statements, hypotheses, generality, and clarity.

### Section II

**T1 (lines 98 to 101).** The discussion of C² versus local Lipschitz continuity is confusing. State one assumption, f in C² on a neighborhood of X. Note that it implies that A is locally Lipschitz. Then say that the oracle needs only a Lipschitz bound on the test function, which Lemma 2 supplies.

**T2 (lines 104 to 108).** State the norm used for ‖δx‖ (Euclidean) and cite \[1\] for the variational bound.

**T3 (equation (4)).** Say how L\_θ is built from the features and how many parameters the class has. For n = 2 and features (1, x\_1, x\_2), three free entries with three features each give nine parameters.

**T4 (equation (5)).** Add a forward reference to lines 302 to 310, where the choice of the trace normalization is justified.

### Section III

**T5 (Definition 2).** State the norm in which L\_h is a Lipschitz constant (Euclidean, to match τ). A free improvement: for a uniform axis-aligned grid, the ∞-norm covering radius is B/(N\_ax − 1) and the ∞-norm Lipschitz constant is Σ\_k D\_k, because |h(x) − h(y)| ≤ Σ\_k D\_k |x\_k − y\_k|. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (Σ\_k D\_k) B/(N\_ax − 1) ≤ (Σ\_k D\_k²)^{1/2} B√n/(N\_ax − 1), so the ∞-norm oracle is never more conservative and removes the factor √n. The proof of Lemma 2(i) already passes through the mixed bound. The change affects Lemma 3 and the extrapolation of Section V-C.

**T6 (Lemma 2).** The lemma is stated for n = 2 only, and nothing in the proof uses n = 2. State it for general n, with the Frobenius sums over all entries, and say that the implementation instantiates n = 2. A framework paper should not restrict its only certified-constant lemma to the plane.

**T7 (Lemma 2(ii)).** The lower trace bound v̲ and the bound Ĝ on sup|g̃\_θ| are introduced inline. Define them in displays. Remark 3 can then use v̲ instead of nα, which gives η = v̲ s ≥ n a s.

**T8 (Lemma 3).** The last sentence ("If s⋆ = 0 < L\_h, this sufficiency argument gives no finite axis count") is the incompleteness of Remark 2 in quantitative form; say so.

**T9 (lines 206 to 222).** This paragraph is important but dense. Move the arithmetic details to an appendix. Keep in the main text the three guarantees a reader needs: exact rational coefficients, outward rounding of every bound, and positive answers only from directed-rounded inequalities.

**T10 (Algorithm 1 and line 404).** The algorithm should list the number of returned violators (60) and the perturbation standard deviation as inputs. The standard deviation is not given anywhere in the paper.

**T11 (Proposition 2).** The second sentence of the proof ("When the grid margin is strict ...") belongs in a remark, not in a proof.

**T12 (Proposition 1, proof).** "Lemma 2 furnishes the explicit constant" holds for the polynomial class only. Add "for the polynomial class; Section V-F treats the neural case".

### Section IV

**T13 (Lemma 4).** Correct and elementary; keep it short. Move the condition "the oracle's Lipschitz constant is produced by a scale-equivariant bounding procedure" into a named assumption, since Definition 3 and Corollary 2 use it again.

**T14 (Corollary 2).** The construction is correct. Two changes. (i) The hypothesis g\_0 < 0 on X means that M\_0 is already a contraction metric at rate λ; say so in the statement, since this is the point of M3. (ii) The hypothesis "every cM\_0 with c ≥ 1 belongs to the family" excludes the implemented class; state this next to the corollary rather than three paragraphs later (lines 291 to 293).

**T15 (Lemma 5).** Correct but close to a tautology. Two requests. (a) Present it as a proposition or an observation with a shorter proof. When sup\_X h\_θ < −ε strictly, the empirical loss is identically zero on a neighborhood of θ (D is finite and h\_θ is continuous in θ), so the gradient is zero and no subgradient selection is needed. The Clarke argument is needed only for the boundary case sup\_X h\_θ = −ε. (b) State whether the implementation resets Adam's moment estimates at each round. If the moments are warm-started, the hypothesis "zero optimizer state" does not match the experiments. The observed stall then needs the explanation in (a): the gradient is exactly zero, so the moments decay to zero within a few steps.

**T16 (Theorem 1).** Correct. A pointwise version with λ\_max(M\_θ(x)) in place of b is available; the global statement is adequate.

**T17 (Theorem 2).** Correct. (i) Assume d in C¹ explicitly. (ii) Give μ\_1 a name ("metric-derivative constant") and a display. (iii) The spectral bound δ\_1 on the residual Jacobian is the practical obstacle, as Remark 4 says. Consider a corollary for the case where the residual is itself a polynomial (bounds from Lemma 1) or lies in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with known derivative bounds, since then δ\_1 is computable.

**T18 (Remark 3).** Correct. Use v̲ when it is available (T7).

### Section V

**T19 (lines 389 to 392).** The sentence on the identity-scaled metric is hard to parse. Suggested wording: "For M = βI, C = β(Aᵀ + A + 2λI). The symmetric part (A + Aᵀ)/2 has eigenvalues −1 ± |x\_1|, so C has eigenvalues 2β(λ − 1 ± |x\_1|) and is indefinite exactly where |x\_1| > 1 − λ = 0.6."

**T20 (lines 387 to 389).** The claim that αI + cJ\_φᵀJ\_φ certifies at rate 0.4 for large c follows from λ\_max(C) ≤ −2c(1 − λ) λ\_min(J\_φᵀJ\_φ) + α λ\_max(Aᵀ + A + 2λI), by Weyl's inequality. Give this inequality and the resulting threshold on c (on X the first factor is smallest at the corners, where λ\_min(J\_φᵀJ\_φ) is about 0.056), so that the reader can verify the claim.

**T21 (lines 450 to 454).** Say how η, b, and μ\_1 were computed (which lemma, which subdivision depth) and label δ\_0 and δ\_1 as certified upper bounds. The exact supremum of ‖∂d/∂x‖\_2 on X is 0.25√18 δ, about 1.06δ, so 1.23δ is a conservative bound. The text should not suggest that the constants are exact.

**T22 (lines 461 to 469).** Describe the trigonometric enclosure in one precise sentence: on each subbox, sin x\_1 and cos x\_1 are enclosed by directed evaluation at the subbox center plus a Lipschitz remainder whose width is proportional to the subbox half-width.

**T23 (lines 482 to 483).** State the output dimension of the network (three entries of the lower-triangular factor) and how M\_θ is assembled from them.

## 5. Experimental comments

The experiments are careful but incomplete: they lack the ablations that would connect the design rules to the outcomes, and they do not report all certified constants.

**E1 (ablations; see M1 and M2).** Run raw versus normalized test, fixed versus coupled margin, and bounded versus unbounded parameters. Present them in one table of certification counts over the same 20 seeds and the same grid.

**E2 (sensitivity).** Report the certification rate as a function of the coupling factor γ (for example 1.1, 1.3, and 2) and of the number of returned violators (for example 10, 60, and all). The value γ = 1.3 is not motivated anywhere.

**E3 (runtimes).** Give one sentence per study with typical learner time and oracle time per round, the number of grid evaluations per round, and the machine. "Timings depend on the machine and verification grid" (line 411) is not a substitute for a number with its machine.

**E4 (Figure 1).** Account for the 20th seed in the first and third regimes (M8). State in the caption that all three regimes train on the normalized test. Use the same labels in the legend and in the text ("fixed margin", "coupled margin").

**E5 (Figure 3).** The figure plots a closed-form formula with one fitted constant and carries little information beyond the three numbers already in the text. Replace it by a sentence or a small table, and use the space for an n = 3 experiment (M5).

**E6 (Figure 4).** The figure is informative. Add a vertical line at δ = δ⋆ and the measured value as δ tends to 0 for reference.

**E7 (Section V-C).** The extrapolation assumes that the slack-to-Lipschitz ratio is independent of n. State this as an assumption, not as an estimate. A quick calculation, to be verified, shows that the ratio is already shape-limited in two dimensions. For the pullback metric cJ\_φᵀJ\_φ at the corner x = (2, 2), λ\_min(J\_φᵀJ\_φ) is about 0.056 and tr J\_φᵀJ\_φ = 18, so the normalized test equals about −1.2 × 0.056/18, which is −3.7 × 10⁻³, for every c. Certifiable ratios in higher dimension may be worse, not equal.

**E8 (neural study).** Report the polynomial oracle's L\_h values next to the neural oracle's 54.1 (M8), so that the reader can see how much of the finer grid is due to enclosure conservatism and how much to the metric itself.

**E9 (Section V-E).** Report the fixed-margin regime for the pendulum as well, or say why it was omitted (lines 475 to 476 say only that it was not run).

**E10 (numbers in the text).** Several numbers (0.0057, 9.9 × 10⁶, 0.0386, 0.00224, 54.1) appear in prose. Make sure every one of them flows from the result files through the macro pipeline, so that the revision does not introduce hand-typed values.

## 7. Presentation and writing

The writing is precise but dense. The abstract is too long, the related work is one block, the caveats are scattered, and a few idioms contradict the plain style of the rest.

**P1 (abstract, lines 2 to 24).** The abstract is about 400 words and lists experimental settings (20 seeds, median of 2 rounds, grid sizes, 13 of 20). Reduce it to 150 to 200 words: the problem, the approach, the three results (soundness with certified constants; failure modes and their remedies; slack-to-guarantee theorems), and one sentence on the experiments.

**P2 (introduction, lines 37 to 67).** The related-work discussion is one block. Split it into (i) contraction certificates, (ii) CEGIS for certificates, (iii) verified neural contraction metrics, and (iv) positioning of this paper. Lines 57 to 62 state the positioning twice; keep one version.

**P3 (contributions, lines 71 to 86).** Order the items by strength: soundness with certified constants; slack-to-guarantee theorems; failure-mode analysis with remedies; experiments. ## 9. A possible reframed contribution statement

The paragraph below is a draft that the author can adapt. The bracketed parts depend on which Priority 1 items the revision includes.

"This paper studies counterexample-guided synthesis of contraction metrics for continuous-time systems with a sound and numerically certified verification oracle. It makes four contributions. First, it gives a grid oracle with certified Lipschitz constants and outward-rounded arithmetic, and it proves that the resulting synthesis loop is sound \[and that the loop terminates in finitely many rounds when the parameter set is bounded and the class is realizable\]. Second, it identifies two failure modes of the loop, a scale degeneracy of the raw contraction test and a stall of fixed-margin training, and it derives the design rules that remove them: a scale-invariant test, a training margin coupled to the oracle requirement, \[and a bound on the parameter set\]. Third, it proves that certified slack converts into an improved contraction rate and into explicit robustness to bounded model error, so that a certificate obtained for a learned model transfers to the true system. Fourth, it reports a controlled experimental study, with a public verified reproduction, on polynomial, trigonometric, and neural metric parametrizations in two \[and three\] dimensions, \[including an ablation that isolates the effect of each design rule\]."

Two remarks on this statement. It places soundness and the slack-to-guarantee theorems before the failure-mode lemmas, which matches their relative weight. It also makes no claim about the neural case beyond what Section V-F shows.
